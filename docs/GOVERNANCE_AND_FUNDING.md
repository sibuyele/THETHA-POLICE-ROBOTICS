# Governance and Funding

## Governance principles
Separate project ownership, technical decisions, safety approval, procurement and financial oversight.

Recommended roles:
- Project sponsor: sets mission and approves scope.
- Engineering lead: owns technical architecture and delivery.
- Independent safety reviewer: can block testing or deployment.
- Privacy and security lead: reviews data and cyber risks.
- Finance and procurement lead: maintains budgets, records and supplier controls.
- Community or public-interest representatives: provide documented feedback and scrutiny.

No person should unilaterally approve their own safety assessment, supplier award and payment.

## Funding controls
For every funding source, record funder identity and verified legal status; amount, currency and date; restrictions and conditions; whether it is a grant, donation, loan, investment or in-kind contribution; conflicts of interest; approved budget; purchase orders; invoices; proof of delivery; actual expenditure; remaining balance; and reporting obligations.

Never describe pledged, requested or proposed funding as money received. Never claim a bank, company, government department or institution is a partner without written confirmation.

## Procurement
Use written requirements, competitive quotes where practical, documented evaluation criteria, conflict declarations, approval thresholds and asset registers. Record sole-source exceptions with reasons. Verify supplier identity and beneficial ownership where legally appropriate.

## Public-sector engagement
Engagement with police, municipalities, emergency services or government must be transparent and properly authorized. Informal conversations or demonstrations do not constitute endorsement, operational approval or procurement commitment.

## Public reporting
Publish periodic summaries of funding received versus requested; budget versus actual expenditure; procurement awards and conflicts; milestones and test outcomes; failures and corrective actions; safety incidents and lessons learned with personal information protected; and independent reviews with unresolved risks.

Changes to mission scope, safety boundaries, data collection, supplier selection or deployment conditions require documented review and approval. Safety reviewers retain stop-work authority.
