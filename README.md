# THETHA Police Robotics

**South African public-safety robotics initiative**  
*Locally engineered. Human-supervised. Publicly accountable.*

THETHA Police Robotics is a proposed initiative to research, design, manufacture, test and support non-weaponized robotic systems for public safety and emergency response.

## Mission
Develop locally maintainable robotic platforms that help trained responders reach hazardous or difficult environments, improve situational awareness, and support people during emergencies—without replacing human judgment or accountability.

## Initial application areas
- Search and rescue, including locating and communicating with missing or trapped people.
- Disaster response, including fire, flood, storm and structural-collapse assessment.
- Emergency assistance, including delivery of first-aid supplies, water and communications equipment.
- Police support, including remote observation, scene documentation and hazardous-object assessment by qualified specialists.
- Training, maintenance, local manufacturing and technical skills development.

## Non-negotiable safety boundaries
- No weapons, weapon mounts, targeting, or autonomous use of force.
- No autonomous decisions to arrest, detain, punish, pursue or physically restrain people.
- A trained human operator remains responsible for every field deployment and consequential action.
- Safe stop, manual override, emergency recovery and loss-of-communications procedures are required.
- Privacy, data minimization, access control, retention limits and audit logs must be designed in from the beginning.
- Independent safety, cybersecurity and operational testing must precede field trials.
- No live deployment or public-sector operational use until relevant legal, regulatory, procurement and safety approvals are documented.

## Project principles
1. Human life and dignity first.
2. South African engineering, skills and responsible local supply chains.
3. Evidence before claims: distinguish concept, prototype, tested capability and operationally approved capability.
4. Transparent funding, procurement, conflicts of interest and expenditure.
5. Community engagement and independent oversight.
6. Interoperability, repairability and lifecycle support.
